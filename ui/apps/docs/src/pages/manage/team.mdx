# Members

A namespace's members are the people who can sign in and see it. Manage them under **Team**.

<Shot id="members" route="/team" of={{ role: "table" }} alt="The Team page showing the Members tab with members and their roles" />

## Adding someone

Invite them from the Team page and assign a role. The role decides what they can do *in the
console* — see [User Roles](/manage/team/user-roles).

On Community the invitation email is never sent, because the instance has no mail. The invitation
is still created and the console gives you its link to copy — send it however you like. Or skip
the person entirely: `user create` and `namespace member add` on the host put them in. See
[Administration](/selfhosted/administration).

## Membership and device access are different things

This is the distinction that matters most on this page, and the one people get wrong.

**Being a member does not grant access to any device.** In identity mode, device access comes
entirely from [access policies](/manage/access-control/access-policies), and a new member matches
none of them.

So adding somebody is two steps:

1. Add them as a member, with a role.
2. Write or extend a policy that grants them something.

A member with no policy can sign in, see the namespace, and connect to nothing. That is deliberate
— default deny means access is something you grant on purpose.

The exception is the namespace owner, who is covered by the starter **Owner access** policy.

## Roles as policy subjects

The cleanest way to handle step 2 is to grant the **role**, not the person:

| Name | Effect | Subject | Devices | Logins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operators to fleet | allow | Role: operator | tag `production` | `deploy` |

Now adding somebody as an operator grants them the fleet, and removing them takes it away, with no
policy edited either time. Per-person policies are for genuine exceptions.

## Removing someone

Removing a member ends their access to the namespace.

Two things to do alongside it:

**Revoke their SSH identities.** Membership and identities are separate records. Revoke the keys
bound to them on the [SSH Identities](/manage/access-control/ssh-identities) page.

**Check for automation on their key.** If a pipeline was configured with a key of theirs, it stops
working — which is exactly why automation should hold a
[service account](/manage/access-control/service-accounts) instead.

<Callout variant="warning">
Neither removing a member nor revoking an identity interrupts a session already open.
Authorization is evaluated when the connection is made.
</Callout>
