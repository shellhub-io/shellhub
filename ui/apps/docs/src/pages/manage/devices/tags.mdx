# Tags

A tag is a namespace-scoped label on a device. Tags are how you stop managing devices one at a
time.

## Why they matter more than they look

A tag is not just a filter for the device list. **Access policies select devices by tag**, which
makes a tag the unit of governance.

A policy granting `tag:production` to the operator role covers every device carrying that tag —
including devices that do not exist yet. Tag a new machine `production` and the grant applies. No
policy is edited, nobody is added to a list.

The alternative is selecting devices by a hostname pattern, which works but is a bet that names
stay consistent. A tag is a decision recorded at enrollment. Prefer tags.

## Applying them

Three ways:

**In the console** — on a device, or to a selection of devices at once.

**Through an install key** — the key applies its tags to everything that enrolls with it. This is
the one that scales: a device arrives already tagged, without anyone touching it. See
[Install Keys](/manage/devices/install-keys).

**Through the API** — for reconciling tags from your own inventory. See
[API Authentication](/api).

## A scheme that works

Tags are free-form, which means the scheme is your problem. What holds up in practice is tagging
along the axes you will actually write rules about:

- **Environment** — `production`, `staging`, `lab`
- **Role** — `gateway`, `sensor`, `kiosk`
- **Location or customer** — `site-north`, `customer-acme`
- **Hardware** — `rpi4`, `imx8`

Then a policy is legible: operators get `production`, the field team gets `customer-acme`, the
firmware team gets `lab`.

What does not hold up is tagging things you already know from elsewhere — the OS and architecture
are reported by the agent, so a tag repeating them is one more thing to keep correct.

## Filtering

The device list filters by tag, which is how you find things in a fleet of any size.

## Renaming and deleting

Tags are namespace-scoped, so renaming one changes it everywhere at once — including inside
policies that select on it. Check what selects a tag before renaming it; a policy pointing at a
tag that no longer exists grants nothing, silently.
