# Single Sign-On (SAML)

SAML lets people sign in to ShellHub with your organization's identity provider instead of a
ShellHub password.

<EditionBanner>
SAML is **Enterprise only**. It is not part of Cloud — this is the one capability where
Cloud is not a superset.
</EditionBanner>

## What it changes

Members authenticate against your IdP, so account lifecycle follows the IdP: somebody who leaves
loses their ShellHub sign-in when their IdP account goes, without anybody remembering to remove
them here.

## What it does not change

**SSO controls sign-in, not device access.** A member who signs in through SAML still needs an
[access policy](/manage/access-control/access-policies) to reach anything, exactly like a member
who signs in with a password.

**SSH identities are separate.** SSO does not distribute or bind SSH keys. Somebody who has signed
in through your IdP still enrolls a key the usual way, through
[an approval at first use](/manage/access-control/login-approvals) or by adding it ahead of time.

That surprises people, so it is worth being direct: SAML is a sign-in mechanism, not an
authorization one. Everything about who reaches which device stays in policies.

## Configuring it

SAML is configured from the Enterprise Admin Console rather than the ordinary namespace settings.

<Shot id="admin-authentication" route="/admin/settings/authentication" edition="enterprise" alt="The Admin Console Authentication page with the Local Authentication and SAML Authentication toggles" />

The page carries toggles for local authentication and SAML, so you can run both while migrating
and switch local off when you are confident.

The self-hosted deployment needs one variable set:

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SHELLHUB_SAML_SECRET` | Encrypts the service provider's X.509 private key |

ShellHub sends the IdP an assertion URL and entity ID that use `http://` unless
`SHELLHUB_AUTO_SSL=true`. When TLS ends at a load balancer or ingress in front of ShellHub, set
`SHELLHUB_PUBLIC_URL=https://shellhub.example.com` so both use `https://`. Register
`https://shellhub.example.com` as the entity ID in your IdP, with no trailing slash. Keycloak
calls this field the client ID.

The Enterprise Admin Console itself is reached with
`SHELLHUB_ENTERPRISE_ADMIN_USERNAME` and `SHELLHUB_ENTERPRISE_ADMIN_PASSWORD`.

<Callout variant="warning">
If SAML appears not to work at all on a self-hosted deployment, check the license
first. Enterprise features sit behind a license check, and an expired or missing one is by far
the most common cause.
</Callout>

## Re-authentication through SAML

On Enterprise, a policy's re-authentication step can be satisfied through SAML rather than the
built-in browser confirmation, so a step-up runs through the same IdP as the original sign-in.

See [Login Approvals](/manage/access-control/login-approvals).

## How the first SAML sign-in finds an account

ShellHub keys a SAML account on the NameID your IdP sends as the subject, not on the email. On a
user's first SAML sign-in no account has that subject yet, so ShellHub looks for one by the email
the IdP asserts:

- **No account has that email.** ShellHub creates one, bound to the subject, with no password.
- **A local account has that email.** ShellHub binds it to the subject and signs the user in. From
  then on that account signs in through SAML without asking for its ShellHub password or MFA code.
  It keeps its password, so it can still sign in locally while local authentication is on.
- **The account with that email is already bound to another subject.** ShellHub refuses the
  sign-in.

This is how existing local users move to SSO without losing their namespaces. It also means that
whoever controls the email your IdP asserts for a person controls that person's ShellHub account,
so map the email attribute to a field your users cannot edit themselves.

## Leaving local authentication on

Keep at least one local administrator account working. If the IdP integration breaks — an expired
certificate, a metadata change — a local account is how you get back in to fix it.

Give that account an email your IdP never asserts. If the IdP sends the same address, the first
SAML sign-in with it binds the administrator account to the IdP user, and from then on whoever the
IdP signs in with that address reaches the account too, alongside the local password.
