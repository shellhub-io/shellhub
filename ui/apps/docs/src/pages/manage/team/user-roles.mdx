# User Roles

Every member of a namespace holds one role. The role decides what they can do to the namespace
itself.

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| **Observer** | Connect to a device, and view device and session details. Nothing else |
| **Operator** | Device-related operations — connecting, viewing details, managing devices — but **not** removing a device. Also has session details |
| **Administrator** | Nearly everything the owner can, but cannot delete the namespace, and has no billing actions |
| **Owner** | Everything |

There is also an internal **service** role, carried by
[service accounts](/manage/access-control/service-accounts). It has no permissions and no
authority — a service account exists to hold SSH identities and connect, not to administer
anything.

## Roles do not grant device access

Worth stating plainly, because the names suggest otherwise.

An **operator** is not automatically allowed to reach any device. Device access comes from
[access policies](/manage/access-control/access-policies), evaluated per connection. A member with
the operator role and no matching policy connects to nothing.

What the role controls is the console: what they see, what they can change, whether they can
remove a device.

## Roles as policy subjects

The two systems meet here, and it is the most useful pattern in the whole model. A policy can name
a **role** as its subject:

| Name | Effect | Subject | Devices | Logins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operators to fleet | allow | Role: operator | tag `production` | `deploy` |

Now the role is doing both jobs: it says what somebody can do in the console, and — through the
policy — what they can reach. Adding a member as an operator grants both at once.

<Callout variant="warning">
A role subject matches by exact name, which means it never matches a service account.
Grant those deliberately. See [Service Accounts](/manage/access-control/service-accounts).
</Callout>

## Choosing

**Observer** for people who need to look — support staff triaging, someone reviewing a session
recording.

**Operator** for the people doing the work. The inability to remove a device is the point: they
can operate the fleet without being able to erase parts of it.

**Administrator** for people who manage the namespace day to day. The two exclusions —
deleting the namespace and billing — are the ones you want to keep to the owner.

**Owner** for exactly one person.

## API keys carry roles too

An API key is created with a role, and it must be **equal to or less than the role of whoever
created it**. An operator cannot mint an administrator key.

See [API Keys](/api/api-keys).
